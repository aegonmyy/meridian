+# Architecture
+
+This document covers the system end to end: why hub and spoke are split the way they are, how the Hub and Spoke contracts are organized into modules, the message protocol between them, where USDC can actually be at any moment, and how share accounting works. It's written for anyone who wants to understand how the pieces fit together before reading about any one mechanism in depth. If you're evaluating the protocol, start here after the README. If you're integrating against it, read this then [docs/withdrawals.md](withdrawals.md) and [docs/security.md](security.md).
+
+## Hub and spoke, and why
+
+Meridian could have been built as one contract per chain, each independently tracking its own share price, with users choosing which chain to deposit on. That's not what it does. There is exactly one accounting authority, the hub on Ethereum, and every other chain is an execution arm with no user-facing accounting of its own.
+
+The reason is that yield opportunities move around. Aave's rate on Arbitrum today is not Aave's rate on Arbitrum next month, and the best rate in the whole system might be on Base tomorrow. If each chain had its own share price, capturing that would mean asking a user to withdraw from one chain's vault and deposit into another's, paying gas and slippage and CCIP latency on every rebalance, and the user would need to actually make that decision. Centralizing accounting on the hub means the protocol can move capital between chains and markets with zero user-facing action, no different, from a user's perspective, than a single-chain vault reallocating between two lending markets. A spoke's job is to receive instructions and hold capital, not to decide anything.
+
+This is also why spokes carry very little logic. A `SpokeVault` doesn't know why the hub sent it a `DEPOSIT`, it doesn't compare rates, it doesn't decide whether to rebalance. It executes what it's told and reports back the truth about what happened. All of the actual allocation decision-making (which markets, how much, when to move) lives off-chain in the agent and on-chain in the `Rebalancer`, one level above the hub.
+
+## Module layout
+
+Both `HUB` and `SpokeVault` were originally single files. Each is now a thin composition of a storage base and three sibling logic modules, with every function's storage layout, external selector, and gas cost verified unchanged across the split. <!-- verified: docs/layout/hub.json, docs/layout/hub-selectors.json, docs/layout/spoke.json, docs/layout/spoke-selectors.json, the pre/post split snapshots -->
+
+```mermaid
+graph TD
+    HS[HubStorage] --> HA[HubAdminModule]
+    HS --> HM[HubMessagingModule]
+    HS --> HW[HubWithdrawalModule]
+    HA --> HUB[HUB]
+    HM --> HUB
+    HW --> HUB
+
+    SS[SpokeStorage] --> SA[SpokeAdminModule]
+    SS --> SH[SpokeHandlersModule]
+    SS --> SC[SpokeConfirmsModule]
+    SA --> SV[SpokeVault]
+    SH --> SV
+    SC --> SV
+```
+<!-- verified: src/hub/HubStorage.sol, src/hub/HubAdminModule.sol, src/hub/HubMessagingModule.sol, src/hub/HubWithdrawalModule.sol, src/Hub.sol; src/spoke/SpokeStorage.sol, src/spoke/SpokeAdminModule.sol, src/spoke/SpokeHandlersModule.sol, src/spoke/SpokeConfirmsModule.sol, src/Spoke.sol -->
+
+Every struct, every state variable, every event lives in the storage base (`HubStorage` or `SpokeStorage`) in its original declaration order. That's the whole trick for why this split is safe: Solidity assigns storage slots by declaration order across the inheritance chain, so as long as one contract owns every declaration and every module inherits that one contract directly, no module can accidentally shift where anything lives. `HUB` and `SpokeVault` themselves end up almost empty, just a constructor that forwards arguments down to the storage base, plus a handful of override-resolution stubs that Solidity's multiple-inheritance rules require when more than one sibling module touches the same base class function (pure delegation, no logic of their own).
+
+The one wrinkle with three sibling modules that don't inherit each other is that a function in one module sometimes needs to call a function that lives in another. Solidity has no notion of "my sibling's public function" unless it's declared somewhere both sides can see. The fix used everywhere here is a hook: the storage base declares a bodiless `virtual` function, exactly one module implements it with `override`, and any other module that needs to call it does so through the inherited declaration. `HubStorage` declares seven of these (`isValidSpoke`, `_idleBalance`, `_allSpokesFresh`, `_newMessageId`, `_requestAllBalanceReports`, `recallFromSpoke`'s three-argument overload, and `attemptSettlement`), `SpokeStorage` declares two (`_aggregatedSpokeBalance`, `_sendOrQueueConfirm`). <!-- verified: src/hub/HubStorage.sol Cross-Module Hooks section, src/spoke/SpokeStorage.sol Cross-Module Hooks section -->

+## Message protocol
+
+All hub-spoke communication is one struct, `CCIPHelpers.CcipMessage`, ABI-encoded into the CCIP `data` field. A `MessageType` enum tells the receiver which handler to route to. <!-- verified: CCIPHelpers.sol:CcipMessage, CCIPHelpers.sol:MessageType -->
+
+| Direction | Type | Carries tokens | Sent by | Handled by |
+|---|---|---|---|---|
+| Hub to spoke | `DEPOSIT` | Yes | `sendToSpoke`, Rebalancer-only | `_handleDeposit` |
+| Hub to spoke | `REBALANCE` | No | `rebalance`, Rebalancer-only | `_handleRebalance` |
+| Hub to spoke | `REPORT_BALANCE` (request) | No | `_requestAllBalanceReports`, Path 2 withdrawal or manual | `_reportBalance` |
+| Hub to spoke | `WITHDRAW_AMOUNT` | No | `recallFromSpoke`, both overloads | `_handleWithdrawalWithAmount` |
+| Spoke to hub | `CONFIRM_RECEIPT` | No | after `_handleDeposit` finishes | `_handleDepositCallback` |
+| Spoke to hub | `CONFIRM_REBALANCE` | No | after `_handleRebalance` finishes | `_handleRebalanceCallback` |
+| Spoke to hub | `REPORT_BALANCE` (response) | No | after `_reportBalance` runs | `_handleReportBalanceCallback` |
+| Spoke to hub | `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL` | Yes, if any pulled | after `_handleWithdrawalWithAmount` finishes | `_handleWithdrawalCallback` |
+<!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:sendToSpoke, HubMessagingModule.sol:rebalance, HubMessagingModule.sol:_requestAllBalanceReports, HubMessagingModule.sol:recallFromSpoke, SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_handleDeposit, SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_handleRebalance, SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_reportBalance, SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_handleWithdrawalWithAmount, HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleDepositCallback, HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleRebalanceCallback, HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleReportBalanceCallback, HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleWithdrawalCallback -->
+
+Note that `REPORT_BALANCE` is one enum value used in both directions, a request from hub and a response from spoke are the same message type, distinguished only by which side is speaking. Every spoke-to-hub message carries `spokeBalance` and `reportTimestamp`, so any interaction with a spoke, not just an explicit balance request, refreshes the hub's view of that spoke.
+
+### Tokens arrive before your code runs
+
+One CCIP fact shapes a large fraction of this design: on a Programmable Token Transfer, the router delivers the attached tokens to the receiving contract *before* calling `_ccipReceive`. By the time `_handleDeposit` runs, the USDC is already sitting in the spoke's balance. The handler never pulls funds from anywhere, it just approves an adapter and calls `deposit`. <!-- verified: SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_handleDeposit, no token pull, only forceApprove + adapter.deposit -->
+
+The same is true in reverse for `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL`: the tokens the spoke sends back are already in CCIP's hands by the time the hub's callback fires, and the hub reads the actual delivered amount from `destTokenAmounts`, the CCIP token envelope, never from a number the spoke merely claims in the payload. This is why the hub can trust its own accounting even if a spoke's message payload is wrong or lying: the amount that matters is the amount CCIP actually delivered, not the amount described in the struct. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleWithdrawalCallback, uint256 actualAmount = destTokenAmounts.length > 0 ? destTokenAmounts[0].amount : 0 -->
+
+## Fund location model
+
+At any moment, USDC that has ever entered the protocol is in exactly one of seven places:
+
+1. Idle on the hub, not yet sent anywhere
+2. In CCIP transit, hub to spoke (`DEPOSIT` in flight)
+3. Idle on a spoke, received but not yet deployed into an adapter (partial deposit skips, recall shortfalls, or direct transfers)
+4. Deployed in that spoke's Aave adapter
+5. Deployed in that spoke's Compound adapter
+6. Deployed in that spoke's Morpho adapter
+7. In CCIP transit, spoke to hub (`CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL` funds in flight back)
+
+The hub doesn't track these seven buckets individually. It tracks three: its own idle balance, `inTransitAssets` (bucket 2 above), and one `spokeBalances[selector]` per spoke that aggregates buckets 3 through 6 for that chain, self-reported by the spoke. Bucket 7 doesn't get its own hub-side counter, it's implicitly still counted inside the old `spokeBalances[selector]` value until the `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL` arrives and the hub applies the spoke's fresh report, which no longer includes what just left. <!-- verified: HubWithdrawalModule.sol:totalManagedAssets, HubStorage.sol:inTransitAssets, HubStorage.sol:spokeBalances -->
+
+The accounting identity `totalAssets() == idle + inTransitAssets + sum(spokeBalances)` holds at every point because a transition between buckets is always paired with the matching counter update in the same transaction:
+
+- Hub sends a `DEPOSIT`: idle decreases, `inTransitAssets` increases by the same amount. Total unchanged. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_sendToSpoke, inTransitAssets += totalAmount inside the same function that dispatches the CCIP send -->
+- `CONFIRM_RECEIPT` arrives: `inTransitAssets` decreases by the exact tracked amount for that message id, `spokeBalances[selector]` is set to the spoke's fresh report (which now includes what arrived, plus or minus any yield or loss). Total reflects the new reality, including anything that happened during transit. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleDepositCallback -->
+- Hub sends `WITHDRAW_AMOUNT`: no hub-side counter moves yet, the funds are still counted inside `spokeBalances[selector]` until they actually leave. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_sendToSpoke, WITHDRAW_AMOUNT carries no tokens outbound -->
+- `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL` arrives: idle increases by the actual delivered amount (via `safeTransfer` inside settlement, or simply the raw balance increase from the incoming token transfer), `spokeBalances[selector]` is set to the spoke's fresh report, which is now lower. Total unchanged, value just moved bucket. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleWithdrawalCallback -->
+
+One layer sits on top of all of this: a spoke's self-reported balance is never applied blindly. Every arriving `spokeBalance` passes through `_applyReportedBalance` first, which checks it against an upside-only sanity band before writing it to `spokeBalances`. A report that looks implausibly large gets quarantined instead. See [docs/security.md](security.md) for the band itself, the accounting identity above assumes a report that passed the check.
+
+## Share accounting
+
+The hub is a standard OpenZeppelin ERC4626 vault. `totalAssets()` returns the sum from the fund location model above, `idle + inTransitAssets + sum(spokeBalances)`, so share price reflects real managed value everywhere in the protocol, including yield accrued on a spoke and value still mid-flight in CCIP. Only `_deposit` and `_withdraw`, the internal hooks, are overridden. The public entry points `deposit`, `mint`, `withdraw`, and `redeem` are untouched, following OpenZeppelin's own guidance against overriding the public-facing pair directly. <!-- verified: HubWithdrawalModule.sol:totalAssets, HubWithdrawalModule.sol:_deposit, HubWithdrawalModule.sol:_withdraw -->
+
+`_deposit` adds no logic beyond the standard mint, USDC in, shares out, at the current share price, gated by `whenNotPaused`. `_withdraw` is fully overridden with no call to `super`, because the standard ERC4626 `_withdraw` burns shares and transfers assets in the same breath, and Meridian's withdrawal engine sometimes can't do both right away. See [docs/withdrawals.md](withdrawals.md) for why and how.
+
+### Worked example: a first deposit
+
+Alice deposits 1,000 USDC into a freshly deployed hub with no other depositors and no spokes registered yet.
+
+- Before: `totalSupply() == 0`, `totalAssets() == 0`.
+- Alice calls `deposit(1000e6, alice)`. ERC4626's `previewDeposit` computes shares as `assets * (totalSupply() + 1) / (totalAssets() + 1)` (the vault uses no decimals offset), which for an empty vault is `1000e6 * 1 / 1 = 1000e6` shares. <!-- verified: lib/openzeppelin-contracts/contracts/token/ERC20/extensions/ERC4626.sol:_convertToShares, no _decimalsOffset override in HubStorage.sol -->
+- `_deposit` runs, USDC moves from Alice to the hub, 1,000e6 shares mint to Alice.
+- After: idle on the hub is 1,000e6, `inTransitAssets` is 0, no spoke has any balance, so `totalAssets() == 1,000e6`. Alice holds all 1,000e6 shares, worth 1,000e6 USDC, a 1:1 price, exactly what you'd expect from the first deposit into an empty vault.
+
+Nothing has moved off Ethereum yet. The USDC sits idle on the hub until the Rebalancer, acting on an allocation proposal from the off-chain agent, calls `sendToSpoke` to deploy some of it. That's a separate action, deposits and allocation are decoupled on purpose, a user's deposit should never block on CCIP latency.
