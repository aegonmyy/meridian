# Resilience

This document specifies the protocol's failure handling design: the rationale for defensive patterns in the code, rather than an assumption of happy-path CCIP delivery. Familiarity with [docs/architecture.md](architecture.md) is assumed, particularly the message protocol these mechanisms operate on.

Assumptions valid for single-chain execution do not hold for cross-chain messaging. On a single chain, a call either succeeds within the current transaction or reverts entirely. Across CCIP, a message may take approximately twenty minutes to arrive, may arrive out of order relative to another message, may fail partway through destination-chain execution and enter a recoverable but non-automatic state, and, absent independent verification, may carry a false self-reported balance. The non-obvious mechanisms in this codebase address four conditions: non-delivery, duplication, delayed arrival, and falsified data.

## Message non-delivery

A message that is stuck rather than failed, never executed or never attempted, requires a recovery path independent of CCIP's own resolution. Three mechanisms address the three locations this condition can occur.

**A pending withdrawal that never settles** (a spoke stops reporting, or a recall leg never arrives) is recovered via `cancelWithdrawal`. After `WITHDRAWAL_TIMEOUT` (24 hours), the withdrawal's owner, and only that owner, may cancel it and reclaim the escrowed shares. See [docs/withdrawals.md](withdrawals.md) for the complete mechanism. <!-- verified: HubStorage.sol:WITHDRAWAL_TIMEOUT = 24 hours, HubWithdrawalModule.sol:cancelWithdrawal -->

**A `DEPOSIT` that is provably dead** (the spoke's `CONFIRM_RECEIPT` will never arrive because the message has permanently failed) is recovered via `reconcileTransit`, a deliberately narrow mechanism. It supersedes an earlier design, `adjustInTransitAssets`, which permitted the owner to write `inTransitAssets` to an arbitrary value with no evidence, bound, or delay, an unbounded write to a value that directly determines share price. `reconcileTransit` releases only a specific tracked message id, by its exact tracked amount, and only after `TRANSIT_RECONCILE_DELAY` (7 days) has elapsed since the message was sent. It cannot invent value or inflate the vault; it can only write down an amount already known to be stuck. <!-- verified: HubAdminModule.sol:reconcileTransit, HubStorage.sol:TRANSIT_RECONCILE_DELAY = 7 days -->

The intended operational sequence attempts CCIP manual execution first; `reconcileTransit` is invoked only once the message is provably dead, confirmed permanent failure with manual execution exhausted. A late `CONFIRM_RECEIPT` arriving after reconciliation has no adverse effect: the tracked amount has already been deleted, so the arrival's decrement subtracts zero and only the spoke's balance is updated.

**An outbound confirm that fails to send** (LINK exhausted, router failure) is queued rather than causing a revert. `_sendOrQueueConfirm` wraps both `getFee` and `ccipSend` in `try`/`catch`. On failure, it persists a `PendingConfirm` record and emits `ConfirmSendFailed`, rather than reverting the handler and unwinding work already completed (an adapter deposit, a balance update). The permissionless `retryConfirm` rebuilds the message with the spoke balance recomputed at retry time, never resending a stale value, and is callable by any address. <!-- verified: SpokeConfirmsModule.sol:_sendOrQueueConfirm, SpokeConfirmsModule.sol:retryConfirm -->

## Message duplication

Message identity in Meridian is derived from a monotonic nonce, not from message content. `_newMessageId` increments `_messageNonce` and includes it in a `keccak256` hash alongside the caller-supplied context, the chain id, and the hub's address. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_newMessageId -->

Content-derived identifiers are subject to collision: two operations in the same block sharing the same amount and receiver produce the same hash if only content is used, causing one operation to silently overwrite the other's bookkeeping and corrupting `inTransitAmount` tracking or a pending withdrawal entry. Including a strictly incrementing nonce in the preimage precludes two operations from sharing an identifier, regardless of any other matching fields.

## Delayed message arrival

A message arriving after the state it applies to has changed must be recognized as stale rather than applied to current state.

A representative case is a Path 3 recall leg's `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL` arriving after its withdrawal has been cancelled or its pending entry otherwise removed. `legToWithdrawal[legId]` is set when a leg is dispatched and is never deleted, including after the withdrawal it belongs to settles or is cancelled. If the mapping entry were deleted, a late arrival for that leg id would fail to resolve to any withdrawal and could be misattributed to an unrelated Rebalancer-driven recall, a category with no `pendingWithdrawal` attached whose arriving tokens are credited directly as idle. Retaining the mapping ensures a late leg arrival always resolves to its true origin. The callback checks whether that withdrawal remains live (`shares > 0`); if not, the arrival is routed to the orphaned-arrival path, `OrphanedRecallArrival` is emitted, and the funds are credited as ordinary idle rather than misattributed. <!-- verified: HubStorage.sol:legToWithdrawal (never deleted by any function), HubMessagingModule.sol:_handleWithdrawalCallback -->

## Falsified spoke reports

A spoke's self-reported balance originates from a contract not fully trusted for accuracy, whether due to a bug, a decoding error, or compromise. `_applyReportedBalance` is the sole entry point through which every balance-carrying callback routes before writing to `spokeBalances`, and it validates the report against a band before acceptance. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_applyReportedBalance, called from all four callback handlers -->

The band is upside-only: `reported <= netSentToSpoke[selector] * (10,000 + MAX_YIELD_BPS) / 10,000 + REPORT_DUST`. `MAX_YIELD_BPS` is 2,000 (20%); `REPORT_DUST` is a flat 100e6 USDC allowance for rounding noise on small spokes. `netSentToSpoke` is the hub's own ledger of funds sent to that spoke via `DEPOSIT`, net of funds returned via `CONFIRM_WITHDRAWAL`, so the ceiling is anchored to hub-tracked state rather than any spoke-claimed value. Under-reporting always passes the check, since a lower value only deflates share price, the direction that requires no validation; a drop exceeding `LOSS_ALERT_BPS` (5%) since the last report emits an informational `SpokeBalanceDropped` event. <!-- verified: HubMessagingModule.sol:_applyReportedBalance, HubStorage.sol:MAX_YIELD_BPS = 2_000, HubStorage.sol:REPORT_DUST = 100e6, HubStorage.sol:LOSS_ALERT_BPS = 500 -->

On breach, the report is not clamped to the ceiling and applied. Clamping produces a fabricated value regardless of direction; trusting the raw value and clamping it both introduce an unverified number into pricing. The report is instead quarantined: `spokeBalances` remains unmodified, the reported value is stored separately in `quarantinedReports`, `SuspiciousSpokeReport` is emitted, and the vault pauses new deposits and withdrawal requests until the owner resolves the quarantine.

Resolution occurs via `acceptQuarantinedReport` or `rejectQuarantinedReport`. Rejection discards the reported value; `spokeBalances` retains its prior value. Acceptance applies the value and rebases `netSentToSpoke` to match it. Without this rebase, the band is fixed and time-independent: a spoke's genuine cumulative yield eventually exceeds a fixed 20% ceiling permanently (approximately two years at 10% APY under the default band), and every subsequent honest report would immediately re-quarantine, a pause-and-accept cycle that does not resolve. Acceptance is an attestation with a durable effect: the owner has independently confirmed the balance is legitimate, and it becomes the new baseline against which subsequent reports are measured, rather than a one-time exception leaving the same ceiling to be exceeded again. <!-- verified: HubAdminModule.sol:acceptQuarantinedReport, HubAdminModule.sol:rejectQuarantinedReport -->

## Defensive receiver principle

A single rule governs most of the resilience mechanisms described above, and the CCIP message handlers generally: a handler must not hard-revert after tokens have arrived or effects have committed. A revert inside `_ccipReceive` unwinds the entire destination execution, including any token transfer already completed within that call frame; this does not strand funds within the receiving contract, it strands the entire message in CCIP's failed-execution state, recoverable only through manual intervention, and unrecoverable if the failure condition is deterministic. The pattern applied throughout the inbound handlers is: attempt each unit of work independently, catch failure at the smallest practical granularity, skip the failed unit while emitting an event describing what was skipped and why, and continue.

**Example: a removed adapter.** The hub sends a `DEPOSIT` instructing the spoke to deploy 300 USDC into a protocol identified by `bytes32("AAVE")`, but that adapter has been removed on the spoke (`exists == false`) after the hub last synchronized its view of the spoke's registry. A handler reverting on the unknown adapter would strand the entire `DEPOSIT`'s tokens, including any other instructions in the same batch, in CCIP's failed-execution state; a handler silently dropping the funds would lose them from accounting. The implemented behavior: `_handleDeposit` checks `exists` per instruction within its loop, skips the missing adapter, emits `DepositInstructionFailed(protocolId, 300e6, "adapter not found")`, and continues to the next instruction. The 300 USDC is not lost: it is already held in the spoke's balance from the token transfer preceding `_ccipReceive`, and remains there as spoke idle rather than being deployed. `_aggregatedSpokeBalance` counts idle alongside every active adapter's balance, so hub accounting remains exact; the funds are reflected in the next `CONFIRM_RECEIPT` regardless of deployment state. <!-- verified: SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_handleDeposit (the exists check and DepositInstructionFailed emission), SpokeHandlersModule.sol:_aggregatedSpokeBalance (idle plus active adapters) -->

[docs/operations.md](operations.md) is the operator-facing complement to this document: LINK balance monitoring, the distinction between a stuck deposit leg and a stuck return leg, and the recovery path for each.
